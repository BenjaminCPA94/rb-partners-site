Finance Law: how to anticipate yearly tax changes for your business

October 2026 5 min read

Each year-end, France's annual finance law reshapes part of the tax landscape that applies to businesses. Rather than discovering new rules once they take effect, it pays to set up a regular watch and anticipate their impact on your activity.

Why the finance law concerns every business

Every year, Parliament votes the finance law, which sets the State budget and often substantially adjusts the tax rules that apply to companies: rates, thresholds, tax credits, reporting obligations. Whatever your size, start-up, SME or subsidiary of a foreign group, some of these measures concern you directly, sometimes from 1 January following their adoption.

The topics that come up most often

From one year to the next, several themes regularly come up in budget debates: corporate income tax, VAT and its special rates, support schemes for investment or innovation, and the taxation of owner-managers and shareholders. Following these broad topic families makes it easier to know where to look first each year.

The gap between the vote and real-world application

A measure voted at year-end does not always take effect immediately: some provisions apply retroactively to the current financial year, others only to financial years opened after a specific date, and others still require an implementing decree before becoming genuinely operational. This gap is a frequent source of confusion, and a vigilance point in its own right.

Keeping watch without spending all your time on it

You do not need to read the text of the law yourself to stay informed. What matters is having a point of contact who identifies, among all the measures passed, the ones that genuinely concern your situation, and who explains their practical impact rather than just their legal content.

This is precisely the role we play for our clients: turning each finance law into a clear list of what changes for you, rather than a pile of texts to decode on your own.
