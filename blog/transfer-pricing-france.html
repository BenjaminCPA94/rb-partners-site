International reporting and transfer pricing: what French subsidiaries need to anticipate

October 2026 6 min read

For a French subsidiary of a foreign group, tax compliance does not stop at national borders. Transactions with the parent company or other group entities, consolidated reporting and transfer-pricing documentation form a specific set of requirements, worth anticipating from the moment the French entity is structured.

Why transfer pricing concerns smaller group entities too

As soon as a French entity carries out transactions with another group company abroad (service recharges, intragroup purchases, royalties, financing), the French tax authority checks that these transactions are priced on terms comparable to what independent companies would agree between themselves. This requirement, often associated with large groups, in fact applies as soon as intragroup flows exist, whatever the size of the subsidiary.

Documenting, not just applying a pricing policy

Beyond setting a reasonable price, companies concerned must be able to justify their transfer-pricing policy through documentation suited to their size and the nature of their operations. This documentation is among the first items requested in the event of a tax audit covering intragroup relations.

Reporting to headquarters: a discipline of its own

Alongside French obligations, the parent company typically expects regular reporting, in its own format and currency, sometimes under accounting standards that differ from French GAAP. Organising this dual exercise, French accounting and group reporting, from the moment the subsidiary is set up avoids having to reconstruct the information after the fact.

Anticipating rather than correcting after the fact

Transfer pricing and international reporting are handled far more simply upstream, when intragroup flows are being defined, than after several years of practice that then need correcting. A regular check-in with a point of contact who understands both French rules and head-office expectations helps secure these topics over time.

This is one of the core roles of our international desk: connecting your French subsidiary's obligations with what your parent company expects, on transfer pricing as on the rest of group reporting.
