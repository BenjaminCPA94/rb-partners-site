How much does an accountant cost in France? What really drives the price.

Accounting fees in France are not regulated: they are agreed with each client and set out in writing in an engagement letter (lettre de mission). Here is what makes them vary, and how to compare two proposals. At Meyvin, a quote follows a first conversation about your actual situation.

The three drivers

What makes a French accountant's fees vary

01

Volume

The number of purchase and sales invoices, bank transactions, accounts and payment methods is the first driver of bookkeeping time.

02

Complexity

VAT regime, cross-border flows, inventory, several entities, reporting to a parent company: each adds review and filing work.

03

Scope

Bookkeeping only, or with payroll, annual legal work, management reporting and advice: the more the firm handles, the broader the engagement — and the less your team has to.

By profile

What a quote covers, depending on your situation

01

Founder-led company, no employees yet

Few documents: the engagement mainly covers bookkeeping, VAT and corporate tax returns and annual accounts.

02

Company with employees in France

Payslips, social filings and annual legal work are added. Payroll is usually priced on the number of payslips.

03

French subsidiary of a foreign group

On top of French compliance come periodic reporting, intercompany reconciliations and exchanges in English with head office.

How we quote

A price agreed in advance, on a written scope

We start with a conversation with a partner to understand your activity, volumes and what you expect from the firm. It is free of obligation.

You then receive a proposal describing the work included, its frequency and the corresponding fees. Once accepted, it becomes the engagement letter.

FAQ

Accountant fees in France: your questions

What is the average cost of an accountant in France?

There is no regulated tariff: fees are freely agreed and vary widely with transaction volume, headcount and scope. A national average therefore says little about your case; a quote based on your activity is the only reliable figure.

Are fees fixed or time-based?

Recurring work (bookkeeping, returns, annual accounts) is generally agreed as a fixed fee, which gives you visibility. One-off assignments are quoted separately.

Is an accountant mandatory for a French company?

Not as a general rule. But keeping compliant books, preparing annual accounts and meeting tax and payroll deadlines in French takes time and technical knowledge — which is what you delegate.

Are accounting fees a deductible expense?

For a company, accounting fees are in principle a deductible expense. The exact treatment depends on your tax position, which we confirm for your case.
